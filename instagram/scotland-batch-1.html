Day 5 GF Tip
GF Travel Tip
Look for the Coeliac UK Accreditation
Restaurants with this certification have verified GF preparation processes and trained staff. It's the gold standard for celiac safety in the UK — and Scotland has more than you'd expect.
GF Gone Global
One thing worth knowing about in the UK: Coeliac UK accreditation. It means the restaurant has verified GF preparation processes, staff trained on celiac specifically, and regular audits. It's not just a sticker — there are actual requirements behind it. In Scotland, we found accredited spots including Browns in Glasgow (5/5 on our GF scale). If you're eating somewhere new and want a quick shortcut to feeling safer, it's always worth asking: "Are you Coeliac UK accredited?"

Day 10 Landmark
GF Gone Global
Isle of Skye, Scotland
The Quiraing
The Quiraing on the Isle of Skye — an ancient landslip on the Trotternish Ridge. The circular walk is about 7km, takes 2-3 hours, and the landscape is genuinely unlike anything else I've seen in Scotland. Wear layers and bring waterproofs. The weather changes fast up here. Afterwards, The Hungry Gull near Portree has a dedicated GF fryer, GF bread for burgers, and staff who understand cross-contamination. 5/5 on our GF scale.

Day 12 GF Tip
GF Packing List
What to Pack for GF Travel in Scotland
Celiac travel card in English (some staff are ESL). GF snack bars for long Highland drives. GF oat cakes — they're a Scottish staple and many aren't safe. And layers. Always layers.
GF Gone Global
What I pack for Scotland as a celiac: A celiac dining card (even in English — some kitchen staff are ESL), GF snack bars for the long Highland drives between towns, and GF oat cakes since traditional Scottish ones often use non-GF oats. Also: waterproof layers, because the weather genuinely changes every 20 minutes, and comfortable walking shoes — you'll cover more ground than you expect. The Highlands can be remote between towns. Bring snacks for the road rather than counting on finding GF options in every village.

Day 13 Landmark
GF Gone Global
Edinburgh, Scotland
Edinburgh Castle
Edinburgh Castle — sitting on a volcanic plug in the middle of the city, with over a thousand years of Scottish history. Book online if you're visiting in summer; the views from the top across the city to the Firth of Forth are worth it. Afterwards, La Locanda on the Royal Mile does scratch-made GF pasta, GF gnocchi, and GF bread. The owner's family is celiac, so the staff really know what they're doing. 4/5 on our GF scale.

Glasgow Cathedral
Glasgow Cathedral — medieval, atmospheric, and free to visit. Glasgow was also better for GF dining than its reputation suggests. 10 restaurants tested, with Red Onion (dedicated GF menu) and Browns (Coeliac UK accredited) both scoring 5/5.
