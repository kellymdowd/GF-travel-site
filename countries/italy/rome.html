Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Easy
Key Phrases (Italian)
“Sono celiaco/celiaca” I have celiac disease
“Questo è senza glutine?” Is this gluten-free?
“Senza glutine, per favore” Gluten-free, please
“Niente pane, per favore” No bread, please
“È infarinato?” Is it floured?
“Contaminazione incrociata” Cross-contamination
Safe Local Dishes
  • Carciofi alla giudia — fried artichokes (Jewish-Roman style)
  • Carciofi alla romana — braised Roman artichokes
  • Abbacchio a scottadito — grilled lamb chops
  • Polenta — cornmeal, naturally GF
  • Panna cotta — cream dessert, usually safe
  • Gelato in coppa — ask for a cup, not a cone
Watch Out For
  • Flour-dusted meats (infarinato)
  • Breadcrumbs (pangrattato)
  • Shared pasta water
  • Farro and orzo (contain gluten)
  • Semolina on pizza peels
Grocery Stores
  • Conad, Carrefour, NaturaSì — free-from aisles
  • Italian pharmacies carry GF products (Schär, Barilla Senza Glutine)
  • AIC app lists certified restaurants across Italy
AIC Note
Italy mandates celiac screening for children. AIC (Associazione Italiana Celiachia) certifies thousands of restaurants. Look for the “Spiga Barrata” crossed-grain symbol on products.

Getting Around

Metro
Lines A (Vatican, Spanish Steps) & B (Colosseum, Tiburtina). Runs 5:30AM–11:30PM (1:30AM Fri/Sat).
BIT single €1.50, Roma 24H €8.50
Bus & Tram
ATAC network covers areas metro doesn’t (Trastevere, Pantheon area). Bus 40/64 to Vatican.
Watch for pickpockets on 40/64
Walking
Historic center extremely walkable. Piazza Navona to Colosseum ~25min. Cobblestones everywhere.
Wear sturdy shoes
Airport
Leonardo Express: Fiumicino → Termini, 32min, €14. Fixed taxi fare €48.
FL1 regional train to Trastevere/Tiburtina €8
Local Tip
The Roma 24H pass (€8.50) pays for itself quickly — grab one at any metro station tabacchi. The historic center has no metro, so you’ll walk most of it — bring comfortable shoes for the cobblestones.

Neighborhoods

Trastevere

Bohemian & Foodie
Ivy-draped medieval lanes, Rome's best restaurant concentration, and liveliest nightlife. Cross Ponte Sisto from Centro Storico.

Centro Storico

Monumental
The heart of Rome — Pantheon, Piazza Navona, Trevi Fountain, Campo de' Fiori. No metro, pure walking.

Vatican / Prati

Grand & Orderly
St. Peter's, Vatican Museums, and the residential Prati grid with wide boulevards and local trattorias.

Monti

Trendy
Rome's hippest neighborhood — vintage boutiques, wine bars, artisan shops. Near the Colosseum on one of the seven hills.

Testaccio

Authentic Foodie
Where Romans eat. Mercato Testaccio covered market, traditional trattorias, and zero tourist veneer.

Travel Tips

  • Say 'sono celiaco/a' — Italians understand celiac disease better than almost any other country thanks to universal childhood screening.
  • Download the AIC Mobile app before your trip — it lists thousands of certified GF restaurants searchable by city.
  • Italian pharmacies (green cross sign) stock excellent GF products — bread, pasta, crackers, cookies. No prescription needed.
  • Book Vatican Museums tickets online for a specific time slot. The skip-the-line difference is enormous.
  • The Roma 24H transit pass (€8.50) is worth it — buy at any tabacchi near a metro station.
  • Drink from the nasoni (small drinking fountains) — Rome's water is excellent and free.
  • Visit the Colosseum and Forum early morning or late afternoon to avoid peak heat and crowds.
  • Gelato tip: if a gelateria has fluorescent-colored mounds, keep walking. Good gelato is stored in covered metal tins.
