GF Friendliness

GF Friendly

New Zealand is one of the most celiac-friendly countries in the world. Its strict FSANZ labeling laws — which require declaration of any gluten-containing ingredient with no minimum threshold — make grocery shopping exceptionally safe, while strong public awareness driven by Coeliac New Zealand means most restaurant staff understand cross-contamination concerns. Traditional Māori and New Zealand cuisine is heavily rooted in naturally gluten-free ingredients like kūmara, seafood, and hāngi-cooked meats, making it easy to eat safely and authentically.

I flew between Auckland, Queenstown, and Wellington, and used the Interislander ferry plus a short train connection to get from Wellington to Marlborough — no car needed for any of that. But the middle stretch, Queenstown to Wanaka to Mt Cook and back to Queenstown, only really works with a rental car; public transit doesn't reach those alpine towns. Worth noting: some trailheads (Rob Roy Glacier near Wanaka, for one) sit down unsealed roads that a lot of rental companies won't insure you on, so check your rental agreement before you rely on one for a hike.
Yes, more than almost anywhere else I've traveled with celiac disease. New Zealand's FSANZ labeling laws require any gluten-containing ingredient to be declared with no minimum threshold, and Coeliac New Zealand's Crossed Grain logo makes packaged food easy to trust at a glance. City scores did vary on this trip: Auckland and Wellington were the strongest at 8/10 each, Marlborough and Wanaka came in at 7/10, Queenstown at 6/10, and Mt Cook Village was the toughest by far at 3/10 — it's a tiny alpine outpost with only four restaurants and no supermarket, so I'd pack in most of your food for that stop rather than count on what's there.
I visited late November through early December — late spring sliding into summer in the Southern Hemisphere, with long daylight for hikes like Roy's Peak and Sealy Tarns and a window before the school-holiday crowds hit over Christmas and New Year. Temperatures still varied a lot by region: Auckland ran a comfortable 20–25°C but breezy, Marlborough's wine country was warmer at 25–30°C, and the alpine towns swung from warm sun to cold wind within the same afternoon. If the high-country tracks are the priority, I'd stick with this shoulder-season window rather than peak summer — you get the daylight without the crowds.
