Getting Around

Tunnelbana
3 lines, 100 stations — many with world-famous art installations. Efficient and covers most of the city.
SL 24hr pass ~175 SEK
Airport Express
Arlanda Express train: 18 min to Central Station (~340 SEK). Flygbussarna airport bus: ~45 min (~130 SEK).
Arlanda Express 18 min
Train to Uppsala
Trains every 30 min, ~38 min journey from Stockholm Central. A great day trip.
From ~65 SEK
Walking & Biking
Compact and walkable. 114 km of cycle paths — Stockholm City Bikes available seasonally.
Transit Tip
Buy an SL Access card (20 SEK) and load a 72-hour pass (350 SEK) — it covers the Tunnelbana, buses, trams, and ferries across Stockholm.

The Royal Palace

Historic
One of Europe's largest palaces with 608 rooms. Baroque architecture with Royal Apartments, Treasury, and Tre Kronor Museum. Catch the changing of the guard at 12:15 PM on weekdays.

Stockholm City Hall

Historic
National Romantic-style landmark and venue for the annual Nobel Prize banquet. The Blue Hall (actually red brick) and Golden Hall with 18 million gold mosaic tiles. Entry by guided tour only.
Kungsholmen
Went here

Gamla Stan

Walk
Stockholm's medieval old town dating to the 13th century. Narrow cobblestone streets, colorful 17th-century facades, Stortorget square, and Mårten Trotzigs Gränd — the city's narrowest alley. Allow half a day to wander.
Central Stockholm
Went here

Nobel Prize Museum

Historic
Covers the history of the Nobel Prize and profiles laureates. Free guided tours in English daily. Housed in the former Stock Exchange Building in Stortorget. Check the cafe chairs — laureates sign the bottom.
Stortorget, Gamla Stan
Went here

Neighborhoods

Gamla Stan

Medieval Heart
Medieval old town on its own island. Cobblestone streets, Royal Palace, Nobel Prize Museum, Stortorget square. Touristy but essential. The city's spiritual center since the 13th century.

Södermalm

Creative & Trendy
Stockholm's hipster hub. Fotografiska museum, SoFo district with independent boutiques and cafes, Monteliusvägen viewpoint for sunset panoramas over Gamla Stan. Strong food and craft beer scene.

Norrmalm

City Center
Stockholm's commercial heart. Drottninggatan pedestrian shopping, NK department store, Kungsträdgården park. Stockholm Central Station is here. Where the action is.

Östermalm

Upscale & Elegant
Beautiful Art Nouveau architecture along Strandvägen boulevard. Östermalms Saluhall food hall (1880s market), Stureplan nightlife. High-end shopping on Nybrogatan.

Djurgården

Museum Island
Green island with Vasa Museum, ABBA Museum, Skansen, Gröna Lund. Oak forests and waterfront paths. Accessible by ferry from Slussen or 20-min walk from Gamla Stan.

Things to Do

Museums & Attractions

Vasa Museum

Paid
Scandinavia's most visited museum. Houses the Vasa, a 17th-century warship that sank on its maiden voyage in 1628. 98% original. Allow 1.5–2 hours. Under 18 free. On Djurgården.
1.5–2 hours ~200 SEK
Book Tickets

ABBA The Museum

Paid
Interactive museum on Djurgården. Virtual costumes, karaoke, original memorabilia. Timed entry required — book in advance. Not included in Stockholm Pass.
1.5–2 hours ~260 SEK
Book Tickets

Fotografiska

Paid
Major photography museum on Södermalm's waterfront. World-class rotating exhibitions. Top-floor bar has panoramic views. Open late some nights.
1.5–2 hours ~200 SEK
Book Tickets

Skansen Open-Air Museum

Paid
World's first open-air museum (1891). 150+ historic buildings showing five centuries of Swedish life. Zoo with Nordic animals — bears, wolves, moose, lynx. Allow half a day.
3–5 hours ~200 SEK
Book Tickets

Travel Tips

  • Sweden is almost entirely cashless — card payments accepted everywhere, even small market stalls.
  • Download the SL app before arriving for easy transit tickets — no need for a physical card.
  • The Stockholm Pass can save money if visiting 3+ museums (includes Vasa, Skansen, Fotografiska, Royal Palace).
  • September is shoulder season — fewer crowds but some archipelago services start reducing schedules.
  • Fika is a cultural institution — find a GF-friendly cafe (Happy Atelier!) and embrace the tradition.
  • Most restaurants have English menus but learning “glutenfri” and “celiaki” goes a long way.
  • The metro art is worth a detour — T-Centralen (blue line), Solna Centrum, and Kungsträdgården are standouts.
What I'd Do Differently
  • Book ABBA Museum tickets further in advance — timed entry sells out.
  • Spend a full day on an archipelago trip instead of just a short cruise.
  • Visit Östermalms Saluhall for the food hall experience — we ran out of time.
