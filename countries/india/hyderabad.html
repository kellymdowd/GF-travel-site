Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"నాకు గ్లుటెన్ అలర్జీ ఉంది" I have a gluten allergy
"గోధుమ పిండి లేకుండా" Without wheat flour
Grocery Stores
  • Search Justdial for "Gluten Free Food Product Retailers" near your hotel — dedicated GF grocery chains are limited in Hyderabad specifically
  • Larger supermarket chains (More, Reliance Fresh) carry some free-from packaged goods — check labels carefully
  • Hotel concierge or imported packaged snacks as a reliable backup
Safe Cuisines
  • Dosa and idli — naturally gluten-free rice and lentil batter
  • Rice-based curries with dal (lentils)
  • Always confirm sambar isn't thickened with wheat flour before ordering
Certification to Look For
India has no formal national gluten-free certification body. The Celiac Society of India is an advocacy and support organization, not a labeling authority — always verify ingredients directly with kitchen staff rather than relying on a certification mark.

Getting Around

Walking
The Old City around Charminar rewards walking — market lanes, mosques, and bazaars sit close together. Elsewhere, Hyderabad is spread out and not especially pedestrian-friendly.
Best for: Old City exploration
Metro
The Hyderabad Metro's three lines are modern and air-conditioned, efficient for reaching key sights and business districts, though coverage of the historic Old City is limited.
Best for: Longer cross-city trips
Auto-Rickshaws & Rideshare
Autos are everywhere — negotiate the fare upfront or book through Ola Auto / Uber Auto for fixed pricing. Uber and Ola cabs are widely available for door-to-door trips, and Rapido bike-taxis are handy for quick, budget-friendly short hops.
Best for: Short hops & convenience
Buses
TSRTC state buses connect the whole city on a budget, though routes can be confusing for first-time visitors.
Best for: Budget travel
Local Tip
Rapido bike-taxis are a good option for quick, budget-friendly short hops when traffic is heavy.

Travel Tips

  • Hyderabad is genuinely one of the easier Indian cities to eat gluten-free in, thanks to two fully dedicated GF restaurants — Only Gluten Free and Millet Ruchulu — a rarity for a metro this size.
  • Even at restaurants that seem safe, always confirm sambar and curry gravies aren't thickened with wheat flour — it's a common, invisible thickener in South Indian and Hyderabadi cooking.
  • Skip street chaat unless you trust the vendor's spice source — Hyderabadi chaat is a particular risk, since chaat masala and bulk spice mixes often contain asafoetida (hing) cut with wheat flour.
  • If you're not near a dedicated GF spot, restaurants inside larger hotels like the ITC are a reliable fallback — trained staff and enough scale to accommodate dietary requests.
