Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Easy
Key Phrases (UK Context)
"I have coeliac disease" Taken more seriously than "gluten-free"
"Can I see the allergen binder?" Required by UK law (Natasha's Law)
"Is the fryer dedicated?" Critical for chips and fried items
"Does the gravy contain flour?" Common hidden gluten source
GF Grocery Stores
  • Marks & Spencer — best overall, "Made Without" range
  • Sainsbury's — strong "Free From" own-brand range
  • Waitrose — premium, curated Free-From selection
  • Tesco — good range in larger stores
  • Whole Foods (Kensington) — US brands not found elsewhere
Naturally GF Dishes
  • Sunday roast (skip Yorkshire pud, ask about gravy)
  • Jacket potato with toppings
  • Full English (skip toast, check sausages for breadcrumbs)
  • Indian curries with rice (avoid naan, samosas)
  • Eton mess & pavlova (meringue-based desserts)

Getting Around

Underground
The Tube is London's backbone — 11 lines covering central and suburbs. Night Tube on Fri/Sat on select lines.
Runs ~5am – midnight
Bus
Extensive 24-hour network. Cash NOT accepted — contactless only. "Hopper" fare: unlimited transfers within 1 hour.
£1.75 flat fare, £5.25 daily cap
River Bus
Thames Clippers serve 24 piers. Scenic way between Greenwich, Tower, Westminster, and Embankment.
Oyster / contactless accepted
Santander Cycles
£2 unlocks unlimited 30-minute rides. Docking stations near most major attractions and piers.
Use the app or docking stations
Visitor Tip
Use contactless (Visa/Mastercard/Apple Pay) instead of buying an Oyster card — same fares with automatic daily capping at £8.90 for Zone 1. The Elizabeth Line runs Heathrow to Liverpool Street in ~40 minutes.

Neighborhoods

City of London Financial & Historic
The financial heart where medieval history meets skyscrapers. Home to the Pan Pacific hotel, Spitalfields Market, and Liverpool Street's restaurant cluster. Walk to the Tower of London and Tower Bridge.
Westminster Monumental
London's monumental core — Big Ben, Houses of Parliament, Buckingham Palace. Cross Westminster Bridge to the South Bank for the London Eye, Borough Market, and The Shard. The best walking corridor in the city.
Notting Hill Charming & Leafy
Pastel Victorian townhouses, Portobello Road Market, and the famous bookshop. Los Mochis Notting Hill brings world-class GF dining. South into Kensington for Hyde Park and Kensington Palace.
Paddington Transport Hub
Gateway to Heathrow via the Express (15 min) and the Elizabeth Line. The Voyage Hotel sits here. Walk to Little Venice for canal-side narrowboat cruises.
Stratford Olympic & Modern
Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park and London Stadium (West Ham). Modern, regenerated East London with excellent facilities and green space. 15 minutes from Liverpool Street on the Elizabeth Line.
Chelsea Affluent & Sporty
Home to Stamford Bridge (Chelsea FC), the King's Road for shopping, and riverside walks along Chelsea Embankment. Fulham Broadway station for the stadium.

Travel Tips

  • Download the Coeliac UK GF Food Checker app — scan barcodes in any UK supermarket to check if a product is gluten-free.
  • Say "I'm coeliac" rather than "I'm gluten-free" — UK staff take the medical condition more seriously than what may seem like a dietary preference.
  • Chain restaurants (Nando's, Wagamama, Pizza Express, Leon) are often more reliable for allergen info than independents, thanks to centralized allergen matrices.
  • UK law requires every food business to provide allergen information — don't hesitate to ask for the allergen binder. It's your legal right under Natasha's Law.
  • Avoid rush hour on the Tube (7:30–9:30am and 5–7pm). Off-peak fares are cheaper and the trains are far more comfortable.
  • The Westminster-to-Tower-Bridge riverside walk is London's best free sightseeing route — about 45 minutes passing the Tate Modern, Shakespeare's Globe, and Borough Market.
  • M&S Food Halls at major stations (Paddington, King's Cross, Waterloo) are your best bet for GF grab-and-go meals when in a hurry.
