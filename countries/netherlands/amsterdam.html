Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"Ik heb coeliakie" I have celiac disease
"Glutenvrij, alstublieft" Gluten-free, please
"Geen gluten, geen tarwe, geen gerst, geen rogge" No gluten, no wheat, no barley, no rye
Grocery Stores
  • Albert Heijn (AH) — the main supermarket chain, with a dedicated "AH Vrij van Gluten" free-from range (bread, croissants, cookies, pasta, stroopwafels)
  • Expect GF products at supermarkets to cost 3–4x more than conventional versions
Safe Cuisines
  • Stamppot — mashed vegetables and potatoes, naturally GF
  • Plain cheese and fresh fish are generally safe bets
  • Ask before ordering sauces or gravies — traditional Dutch versions often use wheat flour as a thickener
Hidden Traps
  • Kroketten & bitterballen — breaded and roux-filled, both wheat-based
  • Bamischijf and other fried Indo-Dutch dishes — wheat noodles plus a wheat breading
  • Soy sauce in Indonesian-Dutch dishes (nasi/bami goreng)
  • Beer batter on fried fish
  • Stroopwafels — most contain wheat; look for a certified GF brand at AH
Certification to Look For
The Nederlandse Coeliakie Vereniging (NCV), founded in 1974, is the Dutch celiac society. Look for the "Crossed Grain" trademark symbol on products, and the "Zorgeloos Glutenvrij" certification on restaurants with verified GF protocols.
Good to Know
English is spoken almost everywhere, so a Dutch celiac card is a nice-to-have rather than a necessity. Apps like Find Me Gluten Free and Gluten Dude are useful for pre-scouting a restaurant's protocols before you sit down.

Getting Around

Walking
Most of Amsterdam's highlights sit within a 30-minute walk of Dam Square, and the compact center rewards wandering on foot.
Best for: The canal ring & city centre
Trams
The main way to get around the city, with trams running every 5–10 minutes and covering most neighborhoods.
Best for: Getting beyond the centre quickly
Metro
A metro network serves the outer districts beyond where the tram lines reach.
Best for: Reaching outlying neighborhoods
Bikes
Bikes are central to daily life here, and Donkey Republic and other services offer easy rentals for visitors.
Best for: Covering ground like a local
Local Tip
The free IJ ferries behind Centraal Station are a great budget tip for crossing the water — no ticket needed.

Travel Tips

  • English is spoken almost everywhere, so a Dutch celiac card is a nice-to-have rather than a necessity — apps like Find Me Gluten Free and Gluten Dude are more useful for pre-scouting a restaurant's protocols before you sit down.
  • Amsterdam's center is largely car-restricted and parking is scarce, so if you're day-tripping in by car like we did, plan to park at the edge of town and tram or walk in.
  • Trams run every 5–10 minutes and cover most neighborhoods — most highlights sit within a 30-minute walk of Dam Square if you'd rather skip transit entirely.
  • Watch for hidden gluten in kroketten and bitterballen (breaded and roux-filled), bamischijf and other fried Indo-Dutch dishes, soy sauce in nasi/bami goreng, and beer batter on fried fish.
  • Stamppot, plain cheese, and fresh fish are reliable naturally-GF fallbacks if you end up somewhere without a dedicated GF menu — just ask before ordering any sauce or gravy, which often use wheat flour as a thickener.
  • Book Anne Frank House tickets well in advance — they routinely sell out weeks ahead, especially in high season.
