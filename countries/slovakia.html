GF Friendliness

Manageable

Slovakia benefits from strong EU allergen labeling laws and well-stocked supermarket free-from sections, making self-catering straightforward in cities. Bratislava offers a reasonable selection of GF-friendly restaurants, but awareness of cross-contamination drops sharply outside the capital. Traditional Slovak cuisine relies heavily on wheat flour — the beloved national dish bryndzové halušky is off-limits — so pack a Slovak-language celiac card and plan ahead for rural areas.

Manageable — I'd put Slovakia at 6/10 overall. EU allergen labeling is solid and supermarkets like Lidl, Billa, and Kaufland stock decent free-from ranges, but traditional cuisine leans heavily on wheat flour (the national dish, bryndzové halušky, is off the table without a GF version), and awareness likely thins out fast once you leave the capital. Bratislava itself scored higher, 7/10, thanks to a handful of solid finds — Funki Punki Pancakes, Pizzeria Primavera, and Sweet Street among them. I only visited Bratislava, so I can't speak to the rest of the country firsthand.
I visited Bratislava in November, and it was cold — temperatures hovering around 2–8°C (35–46°F). Bratislava's own shoulder-season sweet spot is April through June or September through October, which would give you comparable sightseeing weather without the winter chill or peak summer crowds.
