GF Friendliness

GF Friendly

Scotland is one of the best countries in Europe for celiac travelers. Strong UK allergen laws (Natasha's Law) mean restaurants must declare allergens, and most do so willingly. Even small villages on remote islands offer GF bread, dedicated fryers, and staff who understand cross-contamination. Supermarkets across the country stock excellent free-from ranges.

What to Pack for Scotland

Summer in Scotland
Essential
  • Celiac travel card A printed card explaining celiac disease in English. Useful for communicating with kitchen staff — even in an English-speaking country, having it written down helps.
    Essential
  • Portable gluten-free snacks Backup food for long travel days, hikes, and the stretches between towns in the Highlands and on Skye where GF options thin out. Shop recommendation
    Essential
  • Portable phone charger Full days of walking, navigating, and hiking with no outlets in sight drain a battery fast.
    Essential
Scotland-Wide
  • UK power adapter (Type G) UK uses Type G plugs (three big rectangular pins). If you're coming from the US or Europe, you'll need an adapter from day one.
    Scotland
  • Packable rain jacket It will rain, even in July, regardless of which city you're in. Scottish weather changes by the hour — a lightweight shell you can stuff in your bag is the move. A compact umbrella works too for lighter town-day showers. Shop recommendation
    Scotland
  • Layers, layers, layers You'll go from sunshine to wind to drizzle in a single afternoon, in any city. Dress like an onion — t-shirt, light fleece, and that rain jacket.
    Scotland
  • Comfortable walking shoes Cobblestones, hills, and cathedral steps show up in every Scottish city we visited — cushioned soles are non-negotiable by day two. Shop recommendation
    Scotland
For the Highlands & Islands
  • Waterproof hiking boots Non-negotiable for Ben Nevis, the Quiraing, and the Fairy Pools — trails are rocky, muddy, and wet even in summer.
    Highlands
  • Midge repellent Scottish midges are brutal May–September, especially near water and at dawn/dusk in Fort William, on Skye, and around Loch Ness. Smidge brand works best.
    Highlands
  • Reusable water bottle Tap water is safe and excellent throughout Scotland — stay hydrated on the longer hikes.
    Highlands
  • Binoculars For wildlife watching — sea eagles, puffins, and seals near Skye, or scanning Loch Ness from a boat.
    Highlands
  • Sun protection Highland summer days are long (sunrise ~4:30am, sunset ~10pm in July) with real UV exposure on exposed ridge hikes.
    Highlands
