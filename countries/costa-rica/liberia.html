Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"Soy celíaco/a" I am celiac — the priority phrase; it signals kitchen safety protocol more than "sin gluten" alone
"¿Tiene opciones sin gluten?" Do you have gluten-free options?
"¿Tiene salsa de soya, cubitos de caldo, o salsa inglesa?" Does this have soy sauce, bouillon cubes, or Worcestershire sauce? — the 3 main hidden-gluten traps here
Costa Rica's celiac association, Asociación Pro Personas Celiacas de Costa Rica, is a member of AOECS internationally. That said, celiac awareness is generally low outside dedicated establishments — don't expect the fluency in GF dining you'd find in Spain or Italy.
Grocery Stores
  • Auto Mercado — the best GF selection, especially at tourist-area locations like Tamarindo and Playas del Coco; carries Libre Gluten (local brand) and Udi's
  • MaxiPali — a budget option, but with a more limited GF selection
Safe Cuisines
  • Casado — rice, beans, protein, plantains, and salad; naturally GF if the beans aren't cooked with soy sauce or bouillon, so always ask
  • Gallo pinto — confirm no soy sauce went into the beans
  • Patacones / plátanos maduros — fried plantains, naturally GF
  • Fresh grilled seafood and ceviche — just skip any soy-based marinades
Hidden Gluten Traps
  • Soy sauce — all Costa Rican brands contain wheat
  • Bouillon / stock cubes — commonly used in rice and beans
  • Worcestershire sauce ("salsa inglesa")
  • Malt vinegar in some dressings

Getting Around

Car Rental
The standard way to get around Liberia and explore the wider Guanacaste region — this is a spread-out, lower-density area, not a walkable city center. A rental car from the airport gives you the flexibility to reach beach towns, restaurants, and hotels scattered across the region.
Best for: exploring greater Guanacaste
Shuttles & Taxis
Shuttle and taxi services operate from Daniel Oduber Quirós International Airport, though specific current operators weren't verified in this research — ask your hotel to arrange a transfer or confirm pricing before booking.
Best for: airport transfers
Local Tip
Guanacaste is spread out — a rental car is worth the cost if you're venturing beyond the immediate airport area to beach towns like Playas del Coco or Playa Hermosa.

Things to Do

Nature & Wildlife

Rincón de la Vieja National Park

Ticketed
The signature activity near Liberia — a volcanic complex with 12 craters, fumaroles, mud pots, and waterfalls, part of the UNESCO-recognized Guanacaste Conservation Area. Home to over 300 bird species plus cougars, jaguars, and monkeys. Accessed via the Santa María or Pailas ranger stations, either self-drive or with a guided tour. Book through your hotel tour desk or the park's official SINAC channels — no independent booking link is verified here.
Full day ~45 min northeast of Liberia

Africa Mia Wildlife Park

Ticketed
A wildlife park 4km south of the city center featuring African animals in a safari-style setting — an unusual, kid-friendly detour if you're not making it to an African safari any time soon. Book through your hotel tour desk or the park's official channels — no independent booking link is verified here.
Half day 4km south of Liberia

Travel Tips

  • A rental car is genuinely the best way to see the wider Guanacaste region — Liberia itself is a small gateway city, not a walking-tour destination.
  • Say "Soy celíaco/a" first, then ask specifically about soy sauce, bouillon cubes, and Worcestershire sauce ("salsa inglesa") — these are the three most common hidden-gluten traps in Costa Rican cooking, not wheat flour itself.
  • Dry season (December–April) is far more predictable for outdoor activities like Rincón de la Vieja; rainy season (May–November) still works but expect afternoon showers.
  • Auto Mercado has the best grocery-store GF selection, especially at tourist-area locations like Tamarindo and Playas del Coco — stock up there rather than at MaxiPali if you're passing through.
What I'd Do Differently
  • I only had two restaurants from this trip on my radar going in — Posada Real and Monk & Capra — with no personal notes on either. A bit more advance research would have told me Posada Real was a genuine celiac dining gem, and I could have built more of the visit around it instead of finding that out after the fact.
