Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"An bhfuil sé saor ó ghlutin?" Is this gluten-free?
"Tá ceoliac agam" I have celiac disease
"Gluten-free, please" English — universally understood
Celiac Awareness
Ireland has ~50,000 celiacs (one of the highest per-capita rates in Europe). Staff are generally well aware. EU law requires top 14 allergens declared on menus, and Ireland enforces this well. Look for restaurants with the Coeliac Society of Ireland's "Gluten Free Eating Out Pledge."
Grocery Stores
  • Tesco (Deerpark Rd) — excellent GF section
  • SuperValu (Park Rd) — good free-from range
  • Dunnes Stores (New St) — own-brand GF products
  • Aldi (Park Rd) & Lidl — Just Free range
Safe Cuisines
  • Irish stew, colcannon, champ — naturally GF
  • Seafood chowder & smoked fish — ask about thickeners
  • Fresh seafood — Kerry coast catch
GF Bakeries & Cafes
  • Allegro — dedicated GF kitchen, separate facility
  • Petit Delice & Good Boy Coffee — GF options

Getting Around

Walking
Killarney town centre is compact and very walkable. All restaurants, shops, and the national park entrance are within easy walking distance.
Cycling
Popular for Killarney National Park — flat terrain, well-maintained paths. Bike rentals readily available in town.
Killarney Bike Rental
Bus
Hop-On/Hop-Off "Big Red Bus" links town to Ross Castle, Muckross House, and Torc Waterfall.
Taxi
Plentiful in town, rank at College Square. FREE NOW app works. Fares from €4.
From Dublin
Train ~3h15m (€29–40), Bus ~4h20m (€26–35), or drive ~3h17m. Jaunting cars (horse-drawn carriages) are a traditional way to explore the National Park from town — hire from the town centre. Boat tours also depart from Ross Castle across the three lakes.

Travel Tips

  • Rent bikes early — the ride through Killarney National Park to Muckross House and Torc Waterfall is the highlight.
  • Renting a car works well for the Ring of Kerry and Gap of Dunloe if you'd rather go at your own pace — if you'd prefer a guided tour instead, book ahead, especially in summer.
  • Jaunting cars are fun but negotiate the price before getting in.
  • The town is very walkable — you don't need a car unless doing day trips.
  • March weather means layers and rain gear are essential; snow was visible on the peaks.
  • Allegro is reported to have a dedicated GF kitchen — sounds like a great option for safe casual dining, though we didn't get to verify it firsthand.
  • Irish pubs often have live music — check schedules at local pubs.
  • Tesco on Deerpark Road has the best GF selection in town.
What I'd Do Differently
  • Would have spent an extra day to do the Gap of Dunloe tour.
  • Would have eaten at Allegro — didn't discover the dedicated GF kitchen until after the trip.
