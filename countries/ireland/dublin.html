Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Easy
Key Phrases
"I have coeliac disease" Irish/British spelling — staff take it seriously
"Is the chips fryer shared with battered items?" Critical for fish & chips
"Can I see your allergen menu?" Required by EU law in Ireland
Grocery Stores
  • Tesco Ireland — largest free-from range, Jervis Street location convenient
  • Dunnes Stores — St Stephen’s Green location, good own-brand GF
  • SuperValu — strong Irish GF brands
Safe Irish Dishes
  • Colcannon — mashed potatoes with cabbage, naturally GF
  • Smoked salmon — Ireland’s finest, naturally GF
  • Seafood chowder — ask if thickened with flour or potatoes

Getting Around

Luas
Two tram lines: Red (east–west) and Green (north–south through city center). Fast, frequent, covers key tourist areas.
Leap Card for discounted fares
Dublin Bus
Extensive network, 164M passengers/year. BusConnects redesign ongoing.
Leap Visitor Card from €8/day
DART
Suburban rail along the coast. Malahide/Howth to Greystones. Scenic views.
Great for Howth day trip
Walking
Compact, flat city center. Temple Bar to Trinity: 5 min. Most sights within 25 min walk.
Best way to see the city
Local Tip
Get a Leap Visitor Card (€8/day or €16/3-day) for unlimited travel on Dublin Bus, Luas, DART, and Airlink airport bus. Buy at Dublin Airport arrivals or O’Connell Street.

Neighborhoods

Temple Bar

Buzzing & lively
Dublin's cultural quarter on the south bank of the Liffey. Cobblestone streets, live music from every pub, street performers, and the famous Saturday food market.

Georgian Quarter

Elegant & literary
Stunning 18th-century architecture with iconic painted Georgian doors. Home to Merrion Square, the National Gallery, and the Oscar Wilde statue.

Creative Quarter

Trendy & indie
Hub of independent boutiques, specialty coffee, and street art stretching from South William Street to George's Street. Powerscourt Townhouse is the anchor.

Northside

Historic & bustling
O'Connell Street is Dublin's widest boulevard, dominated by the 120m Spire and the GPO — site of the 1916 Easter Rising. Henry Street for shopping.

Liberties

Authentic & gritty
The brewing heartland around St James's Gate. Home to the Guinness Storehouse and Teeling Whiskey Distillery. Old Dublin at its most authentic.

Smithfield

Up-and-coming
Cobblestoned plaza north of the Liffey. Home to Jameson Distillery Bow St. and The Cobblestone — one of Dublin's best pubs for traditional music.

Travel Tips

  • Book Kilmainham Gaol tickets exactly 28 days ahead at midnight — they sell out within hours
  • Order Bulmers (cider) at pubs for a quintessentially Irish GF pint — Guinness is obviously NOT gluten-free
  • Ask for the allergen menu at every restaurant — it’s required by EU law in Ireland
  • Dublin is very walkable — most major attractions are within a 25-minute walk of Temple Bar
  • Download FreeNow app for taxis — Uber works in Dublin but connects to licensed taxis at metered rates
What I'd Do Differently
  • I'd spend more time in the Liberties area beyond just the Guinness Storehouse — it's authentic old Dublin
  • We were in Dublin for St Patrick's Day — an incredible, once-in-a-lifetime experience but the crowds were absolutely insane. If you dislike crowds, skip the 17th and visit the week before or after instead
