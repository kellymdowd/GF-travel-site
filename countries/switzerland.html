GF Friendliness

GF Friendly

Switzerland is a strong destination for celiac travelers, thanks to naturally gluten-free national dishes like rösti, raclette, and fondue, combined with excellent supermarket options from Coop and Migros. Swiss allergen labeling follows EU standards with mandatory restaurant disclosure, and staff at hotels and higher-end establishments tend to be knowledgeable — though awareness can be uneven at casual spots, especially outside major cities.

Switzerland-Wide
  • Power adapter (Type J, 3-pin) Switzerland uses its own Type J socket, different from the rest of Europe — a standard EU plug won't fit in any of these cities.
    Switzerland
  • Swiss francs (CHF) Switzerland isn't in the Eurozone. Cards are widely accepted, but mountain huts and small vendors in Interlaken, Lucerne, and Zermatt in particular may only take cash.
    Switzerland
  • Swiss celiac card from IG Zöliakie IG Zöliakie is Switzerland's national celiac association, and their card is recognized nationwide by restaurants that are IG Zöliakie gastronomy members — not just in one city.
    Switzerland
  • Reusable water bottle Swiss tap water is safe and excellent everywhere I visited — refill freely instead of buying bottled water.

Yes, overall — Switzerland scores 8/10 nationally, helped by strong supermarket infrastructure (Coop's 'Free From!' range and Migros' 'aha!' line are in every city I visited) and the national IG Zöliakie celiac association, whose member restaurants mark GF options clearly. That national score is stronger than any single city, though: Lucerne was my easiest stop at 8/10, while Zermatt's small alpine dining scene and seasonal restaurant closures brought it down to 4/10. I'd treat the country score as a baseline and check the specific city before assuming options will be there.
It depends on the city, and this page actually covers two separate trips. I visited Basel, Geneva, Interlaken, Lucerne, and Zermatt in October 2022, which gave crisp, mild days good for walking and mountain views — except in Zermatt, where October falls in the gap between hiking season and ski season, so several restaurants and hotels were closed. Zurich was a one-night November 2023 stopover on a different trip, and that colder, rainier shoulder season worked fine for a short city visit but I wouldn't build a longer trip around it. For mountain towns like Zermatt or Interlaken, I'd aim for July–September or December–March instead, when everything is actually open.
