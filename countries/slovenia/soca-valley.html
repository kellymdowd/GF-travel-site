Hotel Soča

Bovec town center
Why Stay Here
A really nice hotel in a good location — Felix and the center of Bovec are a short walk away, which matters when you're getting around by bus. I'd happily stay again. Breakfast was a buffet with plenty of naturally gluten-free options (scrambled eggs, smoked salmon, hard-boiled eggs, and more), so I could start hiking days well fed. I also booked my zipline and a canyoning trip through the hotel, which made planning easy.

Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"Imam celiakijo." I have celiac disease
"Brez glutena" Gluten-free
"Ali to vsebuje gluten?" Does this contain gluten?
"Je kuhano ločeno?" Is it cooked separately?
"Brez kruha, prosim." No bread, please
Grocery Stores
  • Spar in Bovec — reportedly a small gluten-free selection, and stock can be thin
  • Stock up beforehand in Ljubljana (Interspar, Mercator, and DM carry Schär and other GF brands)
  • GF tamari packets and trail snacks from home — handy for long hiking days
Safe Cuisines
  • Burgers on GF buns with fries from a dedicated fryer (Felix)
  • Hotel breakfast buffets — eggs, smoked salmon, and other naturally GF staples
  • Grilled Soča trout, a regional specialty — ask that it isn't dusted in flour
  • Polenta and plain potatoes — confirm how they're prepared
Watch Out For
  • Štruklji (rolled dumplings) — wheat-based unless specially made
  • Sauces and soups thickened with flour or stock cubes
  • Breaded fish and schnitzel
Certification to Look For
The crossed-grain symbol, licensed in Slovenia through the Slovenian Celiac Society (Slovensko društvo za celiakijo). Like most places in Slovenia, restaurant menus here generally list allergens (often as numbered codes) under EU rules — handy, but it doesn't tell you anything about cross-contamination, so still ask.

Getting Around

Walking
Bovec is small enough to walk end to end, and my hotel, Felix, and the bus stop were all within a few minutes of each other. Kobarid is similarly compact once you're there.
Best for: getting around town, the Soča Trail
Regional Buses
I got around the whole trip by bus with no car. Arriva buses connect Ljubljana and Bovec (roughly three hours), and local buses run up the valley toward Trenta and down to Kobarid. Off-season, though, the schedule gets thin, and buses can't be tracked live.
Best for: Ljubljana, Trenta, Kobarid
Vršič Pass Bus
A seasonal bus runs over the Vršič Pass between Kranjska Gora and Bovec, passing near the source of the Soča. It's summer season only, so check dates before you plan around it.
Best for: Kranjska Gora, Izvir Soče
Taxis & Shuttles
Local taxis and shuttles fill the gaps the buses leave, which is useful for trailheads like the Kuhinja pasture that are hard to reach by bus outside summer. Book ahead rather than expecting to flag one down.
Best for: trailheads, off-season gaps
Local Tip
Use the bus for one direction and your feet for the other. I took the early-morning bus from Bovec up to the Trenta visitor center and walked the Soča Trail back down the valley — no car needed, and no return timetable to worry about.

Great Soča Gorge

Nature
I stopped here while walking the Soča Trail. The river squeezes through a narrow limestone gorge in that unreal emerald color, and it's absolutely beautiful. It was noticeably more crowded than anywhere else I went in the valley, even in the off-season — but it's still a must.
Near Soča village, on the Soča Trail

Boka Waterfall

Nature
Often billed as the largest waterfall in Slovenia. Sadly, it was pretty dried up when I went in the fall, so I'd set expectations accordingly — it's likely at its most impressive after snowmelt or heavy rain.
West of Bovec, toward Žaga

Izvir Soče (Source of the Soča)

Nature
The spring where the Soča begins, high in the Trenta valley below the Vršič Pass road, and the official start of the Soča Trail. I didn't start my hike here because I needed to be back in Bovec by mid-afternoon. Without a car, getting here is easiest when the summer Vršič bus is running.
Upper Trenta valley
Traveler recommended

Tolminska korita

Nature
A turquoise river gorge near Tolmin with limestone cliffs, caves, and waterfalls along a walking loop. It looked awesome, but I didn't get a chance to visit. It's farther south than Kobarid, and there's an entrance fee.
Near Tolmin

Slap Virje

Nature
A small waterfall spilling into a green pool, a short way west of Bovec near the village of Plužna. I didn't venture over here, but it's close to town if you have a spare couple of hours.

Neighborhoods

Bovec

Adventure base · Where I stayed
The obvious base for the upper valley and where I'd stay again. It's small and walkable, the adventure outfitters (rafting, ziplining, canyoning) are clustered here, and it has the best gluten-free meal in the valley at Felix. Buses run from here up toward Trenta and down to Kobarid.

Kobarid

Food town · ~20 km south
I debated staying here instead of Bovec. I'm glad I picked Bovec, but I rode the bus through Kobarid and it looked adorable — maybe next time. It's the place to base yourself if dining is the priority: Hiša Franko is just outside town and Hiša Polonka is on the main square. It's also closer to Kozjak Waterfall, the Kuhinja pasture, and Tolminska korita.

Trenta

Upper valley · Trails
The scattered settlement in the upper valley inside Triglav National Park, home to the park's visitor center and the source of the Soča. There isn't much in the way of restaurants, so I'd treat it as a day trip rather than a base. The stretch of the Soča Trail from here toward Bovec was the highlight of my time in the valley.

Travel Tips

  • For the Soča Trail, take the early bus from Bovec to the Trenta visitor center and walk back toward the Great Soča Gorge. It's the prettiest stretch, and starting early meant I had it nearly to myself.
  • Off-season bus schedules in the valley are thin, and buses can't be tracked live. Check the timetable (including the ride back) before you commit to a trailhead — it's the reason I skipped the Kuhinja pasture.
  • Book guided activities early in your stay. My canyoning trip was cancelled because I was the only sign-up, and an early booking would have left time to try again.
  • Carry GF tamari packets and hiking snacks. Grocery options in Bovec are limited, and long trail days don't line up neatly with restaurant meals.
  • At Felix, ask for the burger sauce on the side for your fries. Trust me.
  • Waterfalls here depend on the season. Boka was mostly dry when I visited in the fall, so if waterfalls are a priority, a spring or early-summer trip is probably the better bet.
What I'd Do Differently
  • I'd set aside a day for Kobarid — the town itself, Tolminska korita, and ideally a meal at Hiša Franko booked well ahead with a heads-up about celiac.
  • Without a car, I'd plan my days around the bus timetable before arriving, or budget for a pre-booked taxi to the trailheads the buses don't reach off-season.
