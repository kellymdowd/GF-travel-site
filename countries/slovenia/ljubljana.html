Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"Imam celiakijo." I have celiac disease
"Brez glutena" Gluten-free
"Ali to vsebuje gluten?" Does this contain gluten?
"Je kuhano ločeno?" Is it cooked separately?
"Brez kruha, prosim." No bread, please
Grocery Stores
  • Glutines (Stari trg) — a big shelf of GF snacks and groceries alongside the bakery case
  • Mercator and Spar / Interspar — GF sections with pasta, bread and biscuits
  • Hofer — smaller GF range
  • DM drugstores — GF bread and snacks
Safe Cuisines
  • Plain grilled fish and meat (ask about flour dusting)
  • Polenta and buckwheat žganci (confirm preparation)
  • Risotto and salads at places that mark allergens
  • Jota stew (ask whether the stock or thickener contains gluten)
Watch Out For
  • Štruklji and potica — both wheat-based
  • Flour-thickened sauces and stock cubes
  • Breaded and fried dishes from shared fryers
  • Kranjska klobasa and other sausages, which can contain breadcrumbs
Certification to Look For
The crossed grain symbol on packaged foods, backed in Slovenia by the Slovenian Celiac Society (Slovensko društvo za celiakijo). On menus, EU rules mean the 14 major allergens are listed, often as numbered codes, so ask for the allergen list if you don't see it. Like most places in Slovenia, the menus I saw in Ljubljana listed them.

Getting Around

Walking
The center is compact and much of it along the river is car-free, so it's easy to cover on foot. I even walked out to Stožice Stadium for a match.
Best for: Old town, bridges, Tivoli Park
LPP City Buses
City buses run on the rechargeable Urbana card rather than cash, and fares include free transfers for a short window. I took the bus back from the stadium after the game.
Best for: Stožice, BTC City, outer neighborhoods
Intercity Buses
Arriva and Nomago buses leave from the main bus station next to the train station and reach Bled, Bohinj, Kranjska Gora and the Soča Valley. I got around Slovenia by bus (plus a couple of taxis) without a car.
Best for: Onward travel to the Julian Alps
Airport Transfer
Ljubljana Airport is about 25 km north of the city. Bus line 28 runs to the main station, and shared shuttles and taxis are the faster options.
Best for: Arrival and departure days
Local Tip
Don't count on the last bus of the day out of Ljubljana. My evening bus to Kranjska Gora never showed up, and since you can't track buses live, I had no idea until it was too late. I ended up taking an expensive Uber and arriving after dark. Take an earlier departure if you can.

Neighborhoods

Center

Squares, bridges, river cafés
The area around Prešernov trg and the river, with the Triple Bridge, Dragon Bridge and the Central Market. Most of the GF spots on this page are here or a short walk away, including Aurora Slaščičarna, Hood Burger Center and Restavracija Most.

Old Town

Cobbled lanes below the castle
Stari trg and Mestni trg run along the foot of Castle Hill, with baroque buildings, small shops and Locksmith Alley. Glutines is on Stari trg, and the funicular up to the castle starts nearby.

Trnovo & Krakovo

Quiet, residential, riverside
A calmer area just south of the center, with Plečnik's Trnovo Bridge and church, the Botanic Garden, and Špica park along the river. Dežela Okusov, the fully gluten-free restaurant, is on its edge.

Tivoli

Park and museums
Ljubljana's big green space on the west side of the center, with walking paths, the Jakopič Promenade and the National Gallery nearby. Exe Lev sits right on this side of town.

Metelkova

Street art and nightlife
An autonomous cultural zone in former army barracks, covered in murals, with clubs, galleries and the Museum of Contemporary Art (MSUM) next door. It's a few minutes' walk east of the train station.

Frequently Asked Questions

Ljubljana was the easiest place in Slovenia for me to eat gluten-free, and I score it 7/10. There are 100% gluten-free bakeries like Glutines and Aurora Slaščičarna, plus a fully gluten-free restaurant (Dežela Okusov), and most menus list the 14 EU allergens, often as numbered codes. Staff knowledge varies at mixed kitchens, so it still pays to say you have celiac disease and ask about shared fryers and flour-thickened sauces.

Travel Tips

  • Stock up on GF snacks in Ljubljana before heading to the mountains. Glutines has a good grocery shelf, and the selection in towns like Bovec and Bohinj is much thinner.
  • Carry a few GF tamari packets. At Asian restaurants they mean you can say yes to more dishes without relying on the kitchen's soy sauce.
  • Lead with "Imam celiakijo" (I have celiac disease) rather than just "brez glutena." It signals a medical need, not a preference. A written Slovenian celiac card can help too, if that makes you feel more comfortable.
  • Menus usually list allergens as numbered codes. Find the key (gluten is typically number 1 in the EU list) and ask staff to confirm, especially for sauces and anything fried.
  • In Locksmith Alley, look down. The bronze faces are set into the ground and easy to miss.
  • If there's a match on while you're in town, Stožice Stadium is walkable from the center. I walked there and caught the bus back.
What I'd Do Differently
  • I wish I'd gone to Dežela Okusov to eat. It's a fully gluten-free restaurant, and it would have been a much better meal than rushing through DA BU DA before a bus that never came.
  • I'd take an earlier bus to Kranjska Gora rather than the last one of the day.
  • I'd leave time for Ljubljana Castle and Metelkova. I didn't make it to either.
