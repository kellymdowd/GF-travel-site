Where to Stay

Recommended
Where I stayed

Sunrose 7 Gourmet & SPA Hotel

Bohinjska Bistrica
Why Stay Here
A really beautiful, adults-only hotel tucked into Bohinjska Bistrica, the valley's main village. They upgraded me to a room with a windowsill reading nook overlooking the garden, which I loved. I didn't realize until I arrived that it's a digital-detox concept (there is still WiFi, for people like me who can't fully disconnect). The spa is lovely: sauna, jacuzzi, cold shower, and lounge beds with tea, water, and sparkling wine. The real standout was dinner. The kitchen made their four-course tasting menu gluten-free and pescatarian for me, and it was unbelievable. I can't say enough good things about it (I added the wine pairing, and it was so worth it). Breakfast is a good buffet (smoothies, juices, meats, cheeses, traditional dishes, build-your-own yogurt bowls) plus eggs cooked to order. The staff were unbelievably kind. My only regret is that I didn't book a second night to do that dinner again.

Where I stayed

Hotel Bohinj

Ribčev Laz, by the lake
Why Stay Here
Pricey, but it felt worth it. It's set back a little from the lake and right next to the bus stop, which made getting around without a car easy. There's no gym, but my room had a stationary bike and a set of weights, which I loved. The AC is the environmentally friendly kind, so it doesn't get very cool — I was lucky to have a balcony and left the door cracked at night. The wellness area has a salt room, Finnish and Turkish saunas, and a heated outdoor pool. You book a time slot, and it was included with my room, which I appreciated since a lot of other hotels I stayed at in Slovenia charged extra for the sauna. I ate dinner here both nights: GF options are marked and they offer GF bread (honestly, the bread was blah). The trout carpaccio was so good I ordered it both nights, and I liked the vegetable soup. I'd skip the risotto, which was pretty bland.

Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"Imam celiakijo." I have celiac disease
"Brez glutena" Gluten-free
"Ali to vsebuje gluten?" Does this contain gluten?
"Je kuhano ločeno?" Is it cooked separately?
Grocery Stores
  • Mercator and Spar in Bohinjska Bistrica — reportedly small GF shelves (bread, crackers, cookies)
  • Very little near the lake itself, so shop in Bistrica
  • Stock up on GF snacks in Ljubljana before heading up
Safe Cuisines
  • Hotel restaurants — both of mine handled GF well
  • Local trout, simply prepared (ask about flour dusting)
  • Salads and potato sides (confirm dressings and sauces)
Certification to Look For
Menus generally list the 14 EU allergens (often as numbered codes), like most places in Slovenia — gluten is usually allergen no. 1. On packaged food, look for the crossed-grain symbol used by the Slovenian Celiac Society (SDC). Allergen labels tell you what's in a dish, not how it's prepared, so still ask about cross-contamination.

Getting Around

Walking
Ribčev Laz, the lakeshore, the church, and Stara Fužina are all easy walking distance from each other. Walking along the lake is honestly one of the best things to do here.
Best for: The east end of the lake
Buses
I did this whole trip without a car. Buses run from Ljubljana and Bled to Bohinjska Bistrica and on to the lake, and they link the villages along the valley. Hotel Bohinj is right next to a stop.
Best for: Arriving and hopping between villages
Train
Bohinjska Bistrica has a train station on the scenic Bohinj line, which reportedly connects toward Bled and Ljubljana. I didn't use it myself, so check current schedules.
Best for: Getting to Bohinjska Bistrica
Taxis
For the Seven Lakes Valley hike I prebooked a 6:30am taxi from Hotel Bohinj up to Planina Blato. Book ahead — and have a plan for getting back, since cell service on the trail is spotty.
Best for: Early trailhead drop-offs
Local Tip
Bohinj is spread out: Bohinjska Bistrica (where Sunrose 7 is) sits a few kilometers down the valley from the lake. If you want to walk out the door to the water, stay in Ribčev Laz. In summer there's reportedly a seasonal shuttle toward the Seven Lakes trailheads — summer season only, so check dates.

Triglav National Park

Nature
Slovenia's only national park, named for Mount Triglav, the country's highest peak. Lake Bohinj and the Seven Lakes Valley both sit inside it. I spent my big hiking day up here, and the alpine scenery is the real draw of this part of Slovenia.
Surrounds the lake and the high valleys

Church of St. John the Baptist

Historic
The small medieval church (Cerkev sv. Janeza Krstnika) at the east end of the lake, right by the old stone bridge in Ribčev Laz. It's known for the frescoes inside, and it's the classic Bohinj view from the bridge.
Ribčev Laz, by the stone bridge

Savica Waterfall

Nature
A tall waterfall above the west end of the lake that drops into an emerald pool, reached by a short but steep climb up a few hundred steps. There's reportedly a small entry fee. I tried to come down this way after the Seven Lakes hike and got lost, so I never actually saw it.
Above Ukanc, west end of the lake
Traveler recommended

Mostnica Gorge

Walk
A narrow river gorge above Stara Fužina with clear green pools and forest trails, known for an elephant-shaped rock along the way. There's reportedly a small entry fee. I didn't make it here, but it's close enough to the lake to fit into a half day.

Things to Do

Hikes & Trails

Seven Lakes Valley Hike

Free
Honest take: this hike kind of sucked, ha. The valley strings together a chain of glacial lakes (from the Black Lake up past the Double, Kidney, Green, and Brown lakes), and it was much harder than I expected. I prebooked a 6:30am taxi from Hotel Bohinj to Planina Blato, and the trail goes uphill immediately to give you a taste of what's coming. I made it to the Double Lake, but I'd thought I'd downloaded the trail map and it didn't actually work, so I got very lost. I tried to descend via Savica Waterfall, had to backtrack, and finally came down via Vogar — where I was too tired to walk the extra three minutes to the viewpoint. With no return taxi booked and almost no cell service, I walked all the way back to the lake: roughly 30 km, and my legs and feet were hating me. If you go, wear proper hiking boots (maybe even bring poles — the downhills get brutal), make sure your offline map actually works, and plan your ride back.

Zlatorog Fairy Trail

Free
An easy themed walk near Ukanc at the west end of the lake, built around the legend of Zlatorog, the golden-horned chamois of Slovenian folklore. It looks like a gentle option if you want something low-key (or if you're traveling with kids). I didn't get the chance to do it.

A cable car from near the west end of the lake up to the Vogel ski area, with big views over Lake Bohinj and the Julian Alps. It was closed for maintenance when I visited, so check that it's running before you plan a day around it.

Travel Tips

  • Treat the Seven Lakes Valley as a serious mountain day, not a lake stroll. Proper hiking boots make a big difference, and poles may be worth it for the steep descents.
  • Download an offline trail map before you go — and actually open it in airplane mode to make sure it works. Mine didn't, and cell service up there is patchy at best.
  • Prebook your taxi to Planina Blato and think through the return before you start. Without a ride back, I ended up walking all the way down to the lake.
  • Splitting my stay worked well: one night in Bohinjska Bistrica for the Sunrose 7 dinner and spa, then Ribčev Laz to be right by the lake and the bus stop for hiking.
  • Carry a few GF tamari packets. They're handy for plain fish, salads, and rice bowls in places where the sauce situation is unclear.
  • Lakeside restaurants like Kramar get crowded even in the off-season, especially on weekends. Go a little early or late for lunch.
  • Check that the Vogel cable car is running before you build a day around it — it was closed for maintenance when I was there.
What I'd Do Differently
  • I'd go into the Seven Lakes hike with a tested offline map and a return taxi booked. Getting lost and walking back to the lake turned it into a ~30 km day.
  • I'd book a second night at Sunrose 7, mostly so I could have that tasting-menu dinner again.
