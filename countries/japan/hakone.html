Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Difficult
Key Phrases
“小麦アレルギーです” I have a wheat allergy (most effective framing in Japan)
“グルテンフリーですか？” Is this gluten-free?
“醤油なしでお願いします” No soy sauce, please
“小麦粉なしでお願いします” No flour, please
Safe Staples
  • Plain rice (gohan) — always safe
  • Sashimi — bring your own GF soy sauce
  • Kuro-tamago (black eggs) at Owakudani — iconic Hakone snack
  • Onigiri — check labels for 小麦 (wheat)
Watch Out For
  • Regular soy sauce (shoyu) — contains wheat, in almost everything
  • Tempura — wheat batter, ubiquitous in Hakone
  • Udon — wheat noodles
  • Miso soup — may contain barley or wheat-based dashi
  • Rice vinegar (su) — often made with barley or malt in Japan, unlike most US rice vinegar
  • Kewpie mayo — contains gluten in the Japanese version, unlike the GF US version

Getting Around

Hakone Free Pass
All-in-one pass covering Tozan Railway, cable car, ropeway, pirate ship cruise, and buses. The essential Hakone purchase.
2-day from ¥5,000 · Buy at Odakyu stations
Tozan Railway
Japan’s oldest mountain railway from Hakone-Yumoto to Gora — 35 min through switchbacks and forest.
Every 15–20 min · Included in Free Pass
Ropeway
Aerial gondola from Gora to Tōgendai via Owakudani. Volcanic valley views and Mt. Fuji on clear days.
30 min · Included in Free Pass
Sightseeing Cruise
Pirate ship-themed boats crossing Lake Ashi between Tōgendai and Moto-Hakone.
25–40 min · Included in Free Pass
Local Tip
Buy the Hakone Free Pass before leaving Tokyo — it covers virtually all transport in the area and pays for itself in a single day. The classic “Hakone Loop” route goes: Hakone-Yumoto → Gora (train) → Sounzan (cable car) → Owakudani → Tōgendai (ropeway) → Moto-Hakone (pirate ship) → back to Hakone-Yumoto (bus).

Travel Tips

  • Buy the Hakone Free Pass in advance — it covers all transport and pays for itself in a single day on the classic loop route
  • Contact your ryokan at least 1–2 weeks ahead to inform them of celiac/wheat allergy dietary needs
  • Stock up on GF snacks at the FamilyMart near the hotel or at konbini in Odawara before heading into the mountains
  • Say '小麦アレルギーです' (komugi arerugii desu) — 'wheat allergy' is understood far better than 'gluten-free' in Japan
  • Check convenience store labels for 小麦 (wheat) in the allergen box — Japan's labeling laws require wheat disclosure
What I'd Do Differently
  • Actually made it to nukafuku — it was snowing when I arrived and the owner closed early for the day, then it wasn't open again during my trip. Check their hours before you go; they're irregular.
  • Tried Daimasa Honten's sashimi bowls — I hear the fresh seafood is outstanding and the owner is very celiac-aware
  • Spent more time at Owakudani — the volcanic valley deserves at least an hour beyond just buying black eggs
