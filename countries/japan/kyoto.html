Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Key Phrases
"Komugi arerugii ga arimasu"I have a wheat allergy
"Komugi ga haitte imasu ka?"Does this contain wheat?
"Shouyu nashi de onegai shimasu"No soy sauce, please
Grocery Stores
  • 7-Eleven / Lawson / FamilyMart — onigiri (ume, shio-sake), plain rice, boiled eggs, edamame
  • Nishiki Market — rice crackers, fresh fruit, mochi (check for wheat)
  • Daimaru / Takashimaya basements — rice-based sweets, plain grilled items
Safe Japanese Dishes
  • Sashimi — naturally GF, bring your own tamari
  • Onigiri — ume, shio-sake, or kombu fillings
  • Yakitori shio — salt-grilled chicken skewers
  • Yakiniku — plain grilled meat with salt only
  • Mochi & wagashi — rice flour sweets (confirm no soy sauce)
Watch Out For
  • Soy sauce (shoyu) — contains wheat, in almost everything
  • Rice vinegar (su) — often made with barley or malt in Japan, unlike most US rice vinegar
  • Kewpie mayo — contains gluten in the Japanese version, unlike the GF US version
  • Tempura, udon, and most noodle broths — wheat-based

Getting Around

Train
JR lines and private railways (Hankyu, Keihan) connect Kyoto Station to all major areas. JR covers Arashiyama, Inari, and Nara day trips.
Suica IC card accepted
Bus
Kyoto City Bus network covers temples and shrines not on train lines. Bus 100 and 101 hit major tourist spots.
¥230 flat fare · Day pass ¥700
Bicycle
Kyoto is flat and bike-friendly. Rental shops near Kyoto Station and downtown. Great for Higashiyama and Arashiyama.
~¥1000/day rental
Taxi
Clean, reliable taxis with automatic doors. Useful for reaching remote temples or traveling at night.
Starts at ~¥680
Local Tip
Get a Suica IC card at Kyoto Station — it works on JR trains, city buses, and most convenience stores. Saves fumbling for exact change.

Neighborhoods

Gion

Geisha quarter
Kyoto's most iconic district, known for traditional wooden machiya houses, tea houses, and the chance to spot geiko and maiko.

Higashiyama

Temple district
Hillside area home to Kiyomizu-dera, atmospheric walking streets like Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka, and traditional craft shops.

Arashiyama

Nature & temples
Western Kyoto's scenic area with the Bamboo Grove, Monkey Park, and riverside temples surrounded by mountains.

Downtown / Kawaramachi

Shopping & nightlife
Kyoto's commercial heart with Nishiki Market, department stores, and the Pontocho dining alley along the Kamo River.

Kita

Golden temples
Northern district home to Kinkaku-ji and Ryōan-ji, with a quieter, more residential feel away from the tourist center.

Fushimi

Shrines & sake
Southern area famous for Fushimi Inari's torii gates and the Fushimi sake district with brewery tours and tastings.

Travel Tips

  • Carry your celiac card in Japanese everywhere — most restaurant staff don't speak English but will read the card carefully
  • Buy a Suica IC card at Kyoto Station on arrival — it works on trains, buses, and convenience stores
  • Visit Fushimi Inari early morning (definitely before 8am) to avoid the crowds
  • Rent bikes to cover more ground — Kyoto is flat and cycling is the local way to get around
  • Nishiki Market is a great place to find GF-safe snacks like mochi, rice crackers, and fresh fruit
What I'd Do Differently
  • Rented an e-bike instead of a regular bike — the distances between temples are longer than they look
  • Spent more time in the Fushimi sake district — brewery tours offer tastings of naturally GF sake
