Gluten-Free Survival Guide

Easy
Key Phrases
“Soy celíaco/a” I am celiac
“Sin gluten” Gluten-free
“¿Esto es sin gluten?” Is this gluten-free?
“¿Tienen opciones sin gluten?” Do you have GF options?
“¿Puedo ver el menú para celíacos?” Can I see the GF menu?
Grocery Stores
  • El Corte Inglés — excellent gluten-free section
  • Mercadona — Hacendado own-brand, tested and marked sin gluten
  • Carrefour & Carrefour BIO — reliable GF ranges citywide
  • Maná Productos Sin Gluten and L'ATELIER — specialty GF shops
Naturally Safe Dishes
  • Paella and other rice dishes
  • Tortilla de patatas — egg and potato, no flour
  • Patatas bravas — confirm the fryer isn't shared
  • Pulpo a la gallega — octopus, potato, paprika, oil
  • Gazpacho — verify no bread or croutons are added
Gluten Traps
  • Albóndigas — meatballs are bound with breadcrumbs
  • Croquetas — flour-based béchamel, always off-limits
  • Empanadas — wheat pastry
  • Shared fryers — the biggest risk in tapas kitchens
  • Seitan and oats hiding in vegetarian dishes
Certification to Look For
FACE — Federación de Asociaciones de Celíacos de España. Look for their blue certification sticker in restaurant windows and on menus; it means the kitchen has been FACE-verified as safe for celiacs.
Traveler Tips
  • Madrid has an excellent reputation among GF travelers
  • Spain's labeling laws and celiac associations are strong
  • Scan for the blue FACE sticker before you sit down
  • State “Soy celíaco/a” clearly — it's widely understood
  • Always confirm cross-contamination protocols, especially shared fryers

Getting Around

Metro
Madrid's Metro is the fastest way around the city — 13 lines and more than 300 stations, with trains every 2–4 minutes. Central Madrid sits entirely in Zone A, so most trips are a single fare.
6:05am–1:30am daily
Single ticket €1.50–2.00 (Zone A)
Bus
Over 200 bus lines fill in the gaps the Metro misses. Blue buses run inner-city routes; green buses are interurban and head out to the wider Madrid region.
Blue = inner city · Green = interurban
Passes & Arrival Points
If you're riding more than a few times a day, the Tourist Travel Pass covers 1–7 days of unlimited Metro and bus travel and works out cheaper than singles. Madrid-Barajas Airport connects straight into the network on Metro Line 8, and Atocha Station — the main long-distance rail hub for AVE trains to Barcelona, Seville and Valencia — sits a short Metro ride from the centre.

Landmarks & Must-See Spots

5 spots

Puerta del Sol

Historic
Madrid's central square and the literal center of Spain — Kilometre Zero, the point from which all Spanish roads are measured. Home to the iconic Tío Pepe sign and the bear-and-strawberry-tree statue (the city's symbol).
Sol, Centro
Went here

Plaza Mayor

Historic
Madrid's grand 17th-century main square, ringed by red-and-cream apartment buildings with wrought-iron balconies. Historically used for markets, bullfights, and public executions; now full of cafes and street performers.
Centro, a 5-minute walk from Sol
Went here

El Retiro Park

Nature
Madrid's answer to Central Park — a sprawling 19th-century royal garden with a boating lake, the ornate Monument to Alfonso XII, and the striking glass Palacio de Cristal.
Retiro, east of the city centre
Went here

Royal Palace of Madrid

Historic
The largest functioning royal palace in Western Europe, with over 3,000 rooms. The exterior and Plaza de Oriente courtyard are free; touring the state rooms and armory requires a paid ticket.
Palacio, west of Sol
Went here

Teleférico de Madrid

Nature
A cable car linking Parque del Oeste/Rosales to Casa de Campo park across the Manzanares river, with wide views over the city skyline. Currently closed for renovation since 2022, with a planned 2026 reopening — check its status before planning around it. This was ridden during the October 2016 visit, before the closure.
Paseo del Pintor Rosales to Casa de Campo
Went here

Travel Tips

  • Learn the blue FACE sticker on sight. Spain's celiac federation certifies kitchens, and a sticker in the window or on the menu is the fastest signal that staff have been trained on gluten-free preparation.
  • Mercadona is the easy grocery answer. The Hacendado own-brand range is clearly marked for GF — worth double-checking the label each time, since formulations can change sin gluten, which makes stocking up on breakfast and snack food straightforward without hunting down a specialty shop.
  • Still confirm cross-contamination directly with staff, even at certified places. Shared fryers and shared grill space are the usual culprits, and a quick question before ordering resolves most of it.
  • May and October are the months worth aiming for — comfortable temperatures and long, walkable evenings. Skip August if you can; the heat is punishing and much of the city empties out.
  • Book the Santiago Bernabéu tour ahead of your visit. The itinerary is currently reduced for refurbishment, so available slots are more limited than usual.
What I'd Do Differently
  • Would have loved to ride the Teleférico, but it's currently closed for renovation until 2026 — worth checking status before planning around it.
