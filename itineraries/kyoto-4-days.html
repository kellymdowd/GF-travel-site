Day by Day
1
Friday, March 7

Arrival & a Ramen Detour

In by bullet train from Hakone, a hotel right next to the station, and a soy milk ramen spot found by accident after the original plan fell through twice.

Bullet train from Odawara, arrived Kyoto 3:34pm
Stayed: The Thousand Kyoto — right next to Kyoto Station; Gion is the only other area I'd consider

In from Hakone by bullet train, checked into a hotel close enough to the station to make every travel day easier, then spent the evening at the Pokémon Center for photos before dinner. The original plan was Gion Soy Milk Ramen Uno Yokiko — I tried twice, and both times the line was long enough that I gave up, especially since they stop taking orders at 8:30pm even though they're technically open until 10. Ended up at Moon and Back instead, and it turned out to be the better find: genuinely delicious soy milk ramen with no wait. While I was at the station, I also went ahead and bought the return ticket to Tokyo for later in the trip — a Nozomi bullet train, green car, a seat that could fit oversized luggage, and a window on the Mount Fuji side.

Dinner: Moon and Back
Soy milk ramen, found after my first-choice spot didn't work out twice. No wait, genuinely delicious.

2
Saturday, March 8

Fushimi Inari & Cycling Arashiyama

An early visit to Fushimi Inari's torii gates before the crowds, then a morning-long cycling tour through Arashiyama.

Fushimi Inari: 11 min from hotel on the Nara Line, trains run early and often
Cycling tour: met 8:15am at JR Saga Arashiyama Station, north exit

Fushimi Inari first thing — the Nara Line from the hotel is only 11 minutes and runs frequently enough to get there early, well before the tour groups arrive at the famous stretch of torii gates. From there, out to Arashiyama for a cycling tour, leaving the hotel by 7:15am with a stop for onigiri along the way. The tour met at the north exit of JR Saga Arashiyama Station, about 23 minutes out on the San-In Line. Kept the rest of the day low-key after an early, active morning.

3
Sunday, March 9

Tea Ceremony, Soccer & a Sound Bath

A traditional tea ceremony with a garden view near Kiyomizu, an afternoon at a Kyoto Sanga match, and a sound bath to close out the day.

Tea ceremony: 10am, booked via Viator, walkable from the hotel (~42 min)
Kyoto Sanga soccer match: 2pm, tickets booked online in advance

A traditional tea ceremony near Kiyomizu, booked in advance through Viator, with a scenic garden view built into the experience — close enough to walk from the hotel. In between temple visits, an afternoon Kyoto Sanga match gave the day a completely different rhythm — a genuinely local way to spend a few hours that had nothing to do with sightseeing. Closed out the evening with a sound bath booked near the hotel, still working out the exact timing when it was booked but a good way to unwind after an active few days.

4
Monday, March 10 – Tuesday, March 11

Shopping, Temples & Sake Tasting

A slower day of shopping and temples, a custom ring commission, and a sake course before wrapping up loose ends and heading to Osaka.

A quieter day built around walking the city and shopping — including a stop at Glanta for a custom ring — with more temple visits worked in around it. Booked a sake course and tasting through Viator for the afternoon, choosing between an early and a late seating depending on how the rest of the day went. The following morning was for wrapping up anything left undone in Kyoto before the afternoon train out to Osaka.
