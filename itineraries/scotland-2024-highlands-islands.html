2 Weeks in Scotland

July 5–18, 2024 13 Nights · 5 Stops
At a Glance

Thirteen nights through Scotland's central belt, the Highlands, and the Isle of Skye — Glasgow to Fort William to Portree to Inverness to Edinburgh — entirely by train, ferry, and bus. No car required.

This is the trip in full. Each stop links out to its own complete city guide for restaurant and GF safety details — Edinburgh also has its own standalone 3-day itinerary if you're short on time and just want the finale.

Stop by Stop
1
July 5–7 · 2 Nights

Glasgow

A soft landing after the overnight flight — the mural trail, a walkable city center, and Euros quarterfinal energy everywhere.

Flew DFW → JFK → LHR → Glasgow, landed just after 10am
Stayed: The Z Hotel Glasgow, by Queen Street Station

Two nights was the right amount for a soft landing — enough daylight to fight jet lag with a 2pm walking tour, see the mural trail and cathedral, and catch a Euros quarterfinal, before an afternoon train on to the Highlands.

2
July 7–9 · 2 Nights

Fort William

Base camp for Ben Nevis — one big hike day and one recovery day.

Train: Glasgow Queen Street → Fort William
Stayed: Nevis Bank Inn
Ben Nevis trailhead: ~1.4 mi walk from town center

Two nights, not one — Ben Nevis is the UK's highest mountain and a genuine full-day commitment, 5 to 10 hours round trip depending on pace and weather, so the hike sits in the middle of the stay with a travel day cushioning it on each side, rather than heading straight to the trailhead off a train.

3
July 9–13 · 4 Nights

Portree, Isle of Skye

The longest stop on the trip, and worth it — the Quiraing walk, a full-day minibus tour of Skye's scenic points, and days of slack to just wander.

Train Fort William → Mallaig, ferry Mallaig → Armadale, bus to Portree
Stayed: Bracken Hide Hotel

Getting to Skye takes patience — a full travel day of train, ferry, then local bus. Four nights was deliberate once I arrived: the Quiraing walk (an early bus out to Flodigarry, then an 11km loop, 4-5 hours), a full free day to wander Portree, and a guided "Skye in a Day" minibus tour covering the Fairy Pools, Neist Point, Dunvegan Castle, and the Old Man of Storr in one go — worth it just to avoid renting a car for the more remote points.

Full Portree / Skye guide →
4
July 13–15 · 2 Nights

Inverness

A short, easy stop — the Highland Games, a Loch Ness boat trip, and a chance to recover before Edinburgh.

Bus: Portree → Inverness (Scottish CityLink)
Stayed: Royal Highland Hotel

Two nights was the right length for a gateway stop — a Scottish CityLink bus from Portree timed to land right in the middle of the Inverness Highland Games, plus an easy half-day Loch Ness boat trip, with enough slack to recover from a week of hiking before Edinburgh.

July 15–18 · 3 Nights

Edinburgh

The grand finale — Arthur's Seat, Edinburgh Castle, and three of the best celiac-safe dinners of the entire trip.

The last stop, and the one with the most restaurant options by far — Edinburgh's GF scene is genuinely excellent, scoring 10/10 on my GF friendliness scale. I've written up these three days in full detail separately, since they work just as well as their own standalone trip.
